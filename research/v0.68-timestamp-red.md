# v0.68 — Timestamp / Red Channel

## Timestamp hypothesis

We tested time as a possible phase/state input rather than assuming that later puzzles are objectively harder.

High-confidence anchors used:
- PGP key creation: Unix 1325734783 (2012-01-05 03:39:43 UTC)
- Earliest signed message: Unix 1325735163 (2012-01-05 03:46:03 UTC)
- 2013 live-CD signed message: 2013-01-03 00:08:11 UTC
- 2016 signed message: Unix 1451606467 (2016-01-01 00:01:07 UTC)

A family of timestamp residues against moduli 29, 331, 761, 1033 and 3301 was compared with the 16 Page-32 prime ordinals. Randomizing second-of-day while preserving each UTC date gave family-corrected p≈0.872. Result: no timestamp-residue evidence.

Descriptive only: the first signature was created 380 seconds after the PGP key. Page 32 contains prime ordinal 378. The distance of 2 is post-hoc and is not evidence.

The idea that puzzle difficulty increases with time remains a historical-layering hypothesis only; an objective scalar measure of difficulty has not yet been defined.

## Red channel

Independent pixel measurement by Artwaste shows the numbered Liber Primus pages have a genuinely bimodal color channel at glyph level: 13,631 glyphs contain no red and 236 are fully red, with no intermediate glyph class. On page 53 specifically, 132 glyph units have redness 0 and the 51 red glyphs fall between measured redness 171–180, with no glyphs between the two classes.

Therefore our primary treatment of red is **binary red / non-red**, not a multilevel RGB cipher. Exact JPEG intensities are quarantined until original-byte provenance supports interpreting compression-level variation.

Across all 13,867 glyphs, red marks about 1.70% of glyphs. This is a sparse structural channel and is best treated as event/selector information unless stronger evidence emerges.
