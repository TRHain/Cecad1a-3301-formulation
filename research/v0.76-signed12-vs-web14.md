# v0.76 — Signed 12 vs Web 14

## Result
The 12-coordinate PGP-signed OutGuess payload and the archived 14-coordinate webpage use the **same ordering rule: strict descending latitude**.

The two webpage-only rows are:
- Seoul A: `37.577070, 126.813122` — inserted at web position 7.
- Fayetteville: `36.0665472222222, -94.1726416666667` — inserted at web position 9.

Removing those two rows from the 14-row webpage reproduces the signed 12-coordinate order exactly. Conversely, inserting them into the signed 12 at their latitude-sorted positions reconstructs the 14-row webpage order exactly.

## Interpretation
This strongly weakens the hypothesis that the 12+2 split is itself cryptographic key material. The most parsimonious explanation is **list/version augmentation**: the signed image carried a 12-location latitude-sorted list, while the webpage later exposed the same list augmented with two additional latitude-sorted locations.

This does **not** prove the two additions were accidental or operational; it means there is no independent evidence yet that the split is a checksum, selector, fake-path signal, or key.

Continue authentication analysis at the **exact artifact/signature/timestamp/byte** layer, but deprioritize numerical 12+2 theories unless new evidence appears.
